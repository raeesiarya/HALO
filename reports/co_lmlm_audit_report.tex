\section*{Short version}

We ran our three-state causal audit on the public
\texttt{lil-lab/CoLMLM-360M-FW} model and its Wikipedia index. From 1,500
T-REx prompts, 526 form the primary cohort: the unmodified model is correct
and its selected memory entry is verified to support the answer. Under
geometric-plus-value deletion, retrieval-enabled survival falls to
\pct{6.1}. Yet retrieval-disabled answerability is \pct{26.2}, and retrieval
changes a correct control answer into a wrong answer in \pct{21.5} of cases.
Deleting only the selected entry leaves \pct{40.7} intact; geometric closure
leaves \pct{33.3}; value-aware closure leaves \pct{7.0}. Value deletion is
effective but broad, removing a median of 21 and mean of 57.3 entries per
target. A verbatim answer-bearing entry placed just outside the geometric
radius also restores many answers. Our read is that the main open problem is
the unit of deletion: a cosine neighborhood, answer string, source, and
database row are not the same as a fact identity. Identity clustering is not
implemented here. What we have now is the audit layer and a set of baselines
for testing it.

\section{What we ran, and why}

\colmlm externalizes factual spans into a continuous-key, text-value index and
retrieves the top item using a hidden-state query
\cite{feldman2026colmlm}.

The paper already shows effective aggregate forgetting on TOFU. We wanted to
zoom in on the mechanism behind that result. The narrower question is: once a
target entry is removed from a schema-free index, does a correct answer come
from another memory entry, or from the model and prompt without factual
retrieval? That distinction tells us whether the next useful intervention
belongs in the deletion policy, the retrieval policy, or the model.

Our design adapts the causal framework for relational LMLMs
\cite{raeesi2026audit}. For each fact, the model, prompt, decoding, and
parameters are fixed while memory access changes:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.92\textwidth}{p{0.14\textwidth}Y}
\toprule
State & Intervention \\
\midrule
\FULL & Memory unchanged; retrieval enabled. \\
\DELON & Target deletion set hidden; retrieval enabled. \\
\DELOFF & Target deletion set hidden; factual retrieval disabled. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Deletion uses reversible search-time filtering rather than physically
rewriting the index. Let \(C_s(f)\) indicate whether the output in state \(s\)
contains the normalized gold answer or an accepted alias. Conditional on
\FULL\ correctness, we report:

\begin{align}
L(f) &= C_{\DELOFF}(f),
  &&\text{retrieval-disabled answerability},\\
R(f) &= C_{\DELON}(f)\bigl(1-C_{\DELOFF}(f)\bigr),
  &&\text{retrieval-mediated correctness},\\
I(f) &= \bigl(1-C_{\DELON}(f)\bigr)C_{\DELOFF}(f),
  &&\text{retrieval interference},\\
S(f) &= C_{\DELON}(f),
  &&\text{post-deletion survival}.
\end{align}

We additionally inspect \DELON\ traces for retained candidates that support
the target. Trace support is evidence available to the model, but its absence
does not prove weight memorization; a correct answer can also come from the
prompt or general language regularities.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Construction of the auditable cohort.}
\label{tab:coverage}
\begin{tabularx}{0.82\textwidth}{Yrr}
\toprule
Stage & Count & Coverage \\
\midrule
All T-REx prompts & 1,500 & \pct{100.0} \\
\FULL\ produced a selected entry ID & 1,190 & \pct{79.3} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The unmodified model is correct on 763 of 1,500 prompts (\pct{50.9}).
The primary causal cohort is the 526 cases that are both \FULL-correct and
support-verified. The remaining 901 support exclusions comprise 310 prompts
with no selected entry ID and 591 whose selected entry fails the support
judge. All main survival rates are conditional on the 526-case cohort.

We use greedy decoding with at most 12 new tokens on
\texttt{lil-lab/CoLMLM-360M-FW}. The standard policy combines geometric
closure at \(\rho=0.85\) with value support judged inside a top-500 retrieval
envelope and at run time. The completed closures are not truncated. The run
uses the public, approximate Wikipedia index (approximately 240M entries),
not the 2.2B-entry Wikipedia--FineWeb-Edu index
\cite{feldman2026colmlm}. The estimates therefore describe the released
end-to-end system, including approximate search.

\section{What we found}

\subsection{Channel decomposition}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Paired outcomes under geometric-plus-value deletion at
\(\rho=0.85\), restricted to the 526-case primary cohort.}
\label{tab:three-state}
\begin{tabularx}{0.93\textwidth}{Yrr}
\toprule
Outcome & Count & Rate \\
\midrule
Wrong in both \DELON\ and \DELOFF & 381 & \pct{72.4} \\
Correct only in \DELON: retrieval-mediated \(R\) & 7 & \pct{1.3} \\
Correct only in \DELOFF: retrieval interference \(I\) & 113 & \pct{21.5} \\
Correct in both post-deletion states & 25 & \pct{4.8} \\
\midrule
Any \DELON\ correctness: survival \(S\) & 32 & \pct{6.1} \\
Any \DELOFF\ correctness: \(L\) & 138 & \pct{26.2} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The standard policy leaves 32 of 526 answers intact
(\pct{6.1}, Wilson 95\% interval \([4.3,8.5]\)), but only seven
(\pct{1.3}) are correct uniquely with retrieval. In contrast, 138
(\pct{26.2}) are correct without factual retrieval, and in 113
(\pct{21.5}) a surviving retrieval changes that correct control answer into a
wrong answer. All 32 \DELON-correct traces lack retained target-supporting
evidence; 25 are nevertheless also correct in \DELOFF. Thus \pct{6.1} should
not be called retrieval leakage: the retrieval-mediated subset is
\pct{1.3}.

The \DELOFF\ result is stable under a stricter control that forbids the
retrieval token: 132 of 526 are correct (\pct{25.1}), versus 138
(\pct{26.2}) under null retrieval. The paired difference is 1.1 percentage
points (\(p=0.377\)). We call this \emph{retrieval-disabled answerability},
not pure weight memorization, because prompts and general language knowledge
remain available.

\subsection{Deletion policy and scope}

We compare several possible meanings of ``delete this fact.'' Selected-entry
deletion hides only the verified row used in \FULL; provenance deletion hides
its source; geometric deletion hides keys with cosine similarity at least
\(0.85\); value deletion hides retrieved entries judged to support the target
answer; and the combined policies take their union. These are baselines, not
identity clusters.

Removing the selected supporting entry leaves 214 answers intact, and 192 of
those survivors retrieve other target-supporting evidence. Our read is that
deleting one observed row is not yet the same as deleting the fact. Geometry
removes slightly more, but 154 geometric survivors still have gold evidence
in their traces. Value matching drives the large improvement: 141 cases
survive geometry but not value, versus three in the reverse direction
(\(p<5\times10^{-38}\)).

The tradeoff is broad deletion. Value closure removes a
median of 21 and mean of 57.3 observed entries per target, reaching 494 within
a 500-candidate envelope. Common answers can belong to unrelated
propositions, so value closure is a strong efficacy baseline rather than a
precise identity policy. Adding geometry or provenance after value changes
survival only marginally.

\subsection{Geometric boundary stress test}

For each target, this stress test inserts one answer-bearing entry at
similarity \(\rho-\epsilon\), just outside the geometric deletion radius
\(\rho=0.85\). The baseline is geometric deletion alone, whose survival rate
is \pct{33.3}.

An exact answer-bearing value immediately outside the radius is often selected
and restores the answer. We therefore see the cosine threshold as a routing
boundary, not yet a semantic deletion guarantee. Hyphenated, letter-spaced,
and prefix-cued values produce almost no new correct answers (attack gain
\(\leq\pct{0.4}\)). This run supports a verbatim boundary-evasion claim, not
a general paraphrase or obfuscation claim.

\subsection{Secondary diagnostics}

\paragraph{Representation probe.}
A five-fold, fact-disjoint linear probe predicts an accepted answer from
frozen \FULL\ query vectors for 139 of 599 facts (\pct{23.2}), compared with
\pct{6.8} for the most common answer. The overlap with behavioral \DELOFF\
correctness is weak (Jaccard \(\approx\pct{18.3}\)). Query vectors therefore
contain answer-predictive structure, but this is not evidence that they store
complete facts or the same facts recovered behaviorally.

\paragraph{Radius sweep and collateral.}
Geometric efficacy rises from \pct{60.1} at \(\rho=0.95\) to \pct{78.9} at
\(\rho=0.70\); measured collateral reaches only \pct{0.20}. We do not treat
the collateral number as conclusive. Of 526 target neighborhoods, 476 had to
be filled with facts outside the requested cosine ball, and only 564 of 2,747
evaluated links were actually inside it. The entanglement gap is also
degenerate on this grid (469 zeros and 57 ones), while behavior is
non-monotone as deletion exposes different candidates. The neighborhood
definition must be redesigned before using this as a headline result.

\paragraph{Margin predictor.}
We could not estimate margin AUROC in this run. The geometric search stops at
\(\rho\), so the best sub-threshold survivor score is missing. A
threshold-free retrieval envelope is required before this diagnostic is
valid.

\section{What we think is worth testing next}

Let each memory entry be \(e_i=(k_i,v_i,s_i)\), containing a continuous key,
text value, and source metadata. We think the next strong baseline is a
persistent identity assignment
\(z_i\) such that
\[
z_i=z_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e_i \text{ and } e_j \text{ express the same deletable proposition},
\]
under an explicit policy. A deletion request for identity \(z\) should hide
every \(e_i\) with \(z_i=z\), while retaining entries that merely share an
answer, topic, or source.

For example, ``X was born in Paris'' and ``Y was born in Paris'' share an
answer but are different propositions, while ``X's birthplace is Paris'' and
``X was born in the French capital'' may be equivalent. Identity must
therefore combine retrieval geometry with text and provenance rather than use
unconstrained clustering.

\subsection{A minimal identity-aware baseline}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Generate high-recall candidate pairs using key neighbors, value
  overlap, entities, and source links.
  \item Label a benchmark with \emph{same proposition}, \emph{same answer
  only}, \emph{related}, and \emph{unrelated} pairs. Shared-answer cases are
  essential hard negatives.
  \item Score proposition equivalence using bidirectional entailment,
  entity/relation consistency, context, provenance, and key similarity.
  Form constrained clusters and store an inspectable, persistent identity ID.
  \item Delete atomically by identity and run the existing three-state audit
  without changing the evaluation cohort or decoding.
\end{enumerate}

The comparison we would care about is concrete: can this approach get close to
value closure's \pct{93.0} efficacy while deleting far fewer than its mean of
57.3 entries, with high human-validated cluster precision and low behavioral
collateral? We would keep selected-entry, provenance, geometric, value,
geometric-plus-value, hybrid, and a small manually verified semantic oracle as
the baselines. We would report \(L\), \(R\), \(I\), trace evidence, cluster
precision/recall, cluster size, latency, and collateral on separate
hard-negative sets for shared answer, entity, relation, source, and key
neighborhood. Splits should be identity-disjoint.

Separately, two follow-ups look useful. First, calibrated abstention could help
when deletion leaves only weak or conflicting evidence; the interference rate
shows that blindly accepting the next candidate can hurt. Second, we should
repair the margin and collateral diagnostics, extend the boundary test to
natural paraphrases and distributed copies, and then repeat the locked
protocol on the 2.2B-entry combined index.

\section{What this note does not claim}

To keep the interpretation clean:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Results are conditional on 526 \FULL-correct, support-verified T-REx
  prompts, not all knowledge in the model or index.
  \item Correctness is normalized answer containment; the automated support
  judge and value predicate still require human validation.
  \item \DELOFF\ removes factual retrieval but does not separate parameters
  from information in the prompt.
  \item Search-time filtering tests behavioral deletion, not physical erasure,
  replicas, caches, or later index rebuilds.
  \item Approximate search, the 500-candidate value envelope, and the observed
  query limit any claim of exhaustive semantic deletion.
  \item Identity clustering, modified pretraining, and learned abstention have
  not yet been implemented.
\end{itemize}

\section{Our overall read}

Our overall read is positive for the \colmlm\ direction: the external memory
is editable, inspectable, and clearly doing real work. The part that still
looks open is the semantic unit of deletion. Selected-entry and geometric
policies miss many alternative answer-bearing spans; value-aware deletion
closes most paths but is too broad to serve as an identity rule. We now have
the paired interventions, traces, controls, and baselines needed to test the
next step together: proposition-level identity clusters that support atomic
deletion with measured efficacy and collateral damage.
