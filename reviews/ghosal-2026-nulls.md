# NULLs: Natively Unlearnable Large Language Models

## 1. Citation

Gaurav Ghosal, Pratyush Maini, Aditi Raghunathan.
*Natively Unlearnable Large Language Models.* arXiv:2606.13873v1 [cs.LG], 11 Jun 2026. Preprint (no venue yet).
Code: https://github.com/AR-FORUM/NULLS · Checkpoints: https://huggingface.co/collections/gauravrghosal/nulls

## 2. One-paragraph summary

NULLs trains a transformer so that each pretraining *source* (e.g. one Wikipedia
article) writes its unique knowledge into a sparse, source-specific subset of MLP
"sink" neurons, while cross-source knowledge accumulates in a small always-active
shared backbone. Unlearning a source at deployment is then just refusing to
activate its sink mask — no gradient updates, no access to retained data. On a 1B
model trained on Wikipedia with ~6M article-level sources, deactivating an
article's sink matches gold-standard retraining: article-unique facts collapse,
shared and inferable facts survive. A Harry Potter case study shows the removal
resists GCG-style adversarial prompting and relearning attacks that reverse
post-hoc unlearning within ~10 finetuning steps, and downstream benchmarks show
no general-capability cost versus a parameter-matched standard transformer.

## 3. Claims (ranked by load-bearing weight)

1. **Surgical source removal:** deactivating one article's sink removes that
   article's unique facts while preserving shared/inferred facts, closely
   matching retraining without the article (Fig. 3, 4).
2. **Robustness:** sink deactivation resists adversarial extraction (ACR ≈
   retrained) and relearning attacks, unlike post-hoc NPO/gradient ascent
   (Fig. 7).
3. **No capability tax:** NULLs matches a standard transformer on knowledge
   capacity and 4 downstream benchmarks (Fig. 8c, Table 1).
4. **Scalability in sources:** deterministic pseudo-random masks over a fixed
   sink pool give combinatorially many sources without parameter growth
   (mechanism claim; demonstrated at 6M sources).

## 4. Method

- **Architecture:** LLaMA-style transformer; only the MLP is modified. Hidden
  neurons at each layer are split into a shared backbone of N_gen = 500 neurons
  (always active) and a sink pool of N_pool = 8000. Each source gets a
  deterministic sparse mask (N_source = 100 active sinks, overlap ratio
  N_source/N_pool = 0.013) generated by seeding a PRNG with the source
  identifier — masks are generated on the fly, nothing is stored per source.
  Forward pass: `x = x * mask(source_id)` after the SiLU gate (Fig. 2).
- **Why it works:** inherits the memorization-sinks dynamic (Ghosal et al. 2025,
  arXiv:2507.09937). Source-specific facts get low-interference gradient signal
  in the sinks and fit there first; once fit, gradient pressure on the backbone
  vanishes and backbone leakage decays, so the backbone retains only
  cross-source-reinforced information.
- **Inference:** Sink-On = activate ground-truth source mask; Sink-Off =
  activate the *next-closest* source's mask (by cosine similarity of mean-pooled
  source embeddings). Cross-document attention masking prevents leakage when a
  training context spans documents. Multi-source prompts are routed to a single
  nearest sink (acknowledged limitation, App. A.2).
- **Training:** Wikipedia setting: 1B params, 7 epochs ≈ 32B tokens, SmolLM2
  backbone geometry (n_embd 960, 32 layers, SmolLM2-360M tokenizer) with MLP
  widened to 8192; sources = ~6M article titles. Harry Potter setting: 1B on
  3.8B tokens of C4 + 7 HP books, sources = 5000 semantic C4 clusters + 1 HP
  cluster. AdamW, lr 5e-4, bf16 (App. A.3).

## 5. Evidence

| Claim | Experiment | Result | Fairness notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surgical removal | Truth Ratio (TR) on 200 facts/category, Sink-On vs Sink-Off vs Retrain | Unique facts: TR collapses under Sink-Off, matching retrain; inferred/shared facts preserved (Fig. 4) | Retrain baseline is the right gold standard; only 200 facts per category, article count for the retrain comparison not stated; no seeds/error bars |
| Post-hoc methods fail | NPO + gradient ascent up to 5 epochs on target article | Degrade shared and article-specific TR at similar rates (Fig. 3c) | Fair qualitatively; hyperparameter tuning effort for baselines not described |
| Adversarial robustness | GCG prompts, Adversarial Compression Ratio | Sink-Off ACR ≈ Retrained ≈ 1; Sink-On ≈ 1.8 (Fig. 7a) | Standard metric (Schwarzschild et al. 2024) |
| Relearning robustness | Finetune on held-out HP subset | NULLs Sink-Off tracks Retrained; NPO reversed in <10 steps (Fig. 7b) | Matches known post-hoc fragility (Fan et al. 2025) |
| No capability tax | ARC-E, Winogrande, PIQA, SciQ + TR vs param-matched transformer | Avg 0.560 vs 0.555, within 1 SD (Table 1, Fig. 8c) | Only 4 benchmarks, 1B scale; TR parity on Wikipedia cloze |
| Scaling knobs | Overlap-ratio and N_source sweeps (25% Wiki subset) | Lower overlap → more memorization; higher overlap → mild backbone leakage; N_source scales capacity without hurting unlearnability (Fig. 8a,b) | Subset only; useful design guidance |

Evaluation is likelihood-based (Truth Ratio: geometric-mean-normalized odds of
correct vs GPT-5-generated plausible distractors on cloze prompts), not
generation-based.

## 6. Strengths

- The two-requirements framing (disentanglement vs joint learning) is crisp, and
  the mechanism resolves it with a one-line architectural change — no per-source
  parameters, no routing table, masks derivable from the source id alone.
- Gold-standard retraining comparison at article granularity, plus the
  shared/unique/inferred fact taxonomy (with semantic dedup), is exactly the
  right evaluation decomposition for collateral damage.
- Adversarial evaluation (GCG extraction + relearning attack) addresses the two
  best-known failure modes of post-hoc unlearning head-on.
- 6M sources demonstrates the combinatorial-mask claim at realistic granularity.
- Honest limitations section; hyperparameters fully specified; code and
  checkpoints released (litgpt-based).

## 7. Weaknesses & limitations

- **Single run, no error bars** anywhere except Table 1; Fig. 3/4 rest on 200
  facts per category from one training run. How many target articles the
  retraining comparison covers is not stated (retraining per article is
  expensive, so presumably few).
- **Sources must be defined at training time.** Real takedown requests
  (copyright by author/publisher, GDPR by person) rarely align with article
  boundaries; the paper acknowledges this but doesn't test mismatched requests.
- **Routing is a soft spot.** Deployment-time sink selection is nearest-neighbor
  over mean-pooled source embeddings; an adversary who controls the prompt may
  influence routing, and multi-source prompts are unhandled. Robustness of the
  *router* (as opposed to the mask mechanism) is untested.
- **Sink-Off ≠ removal.** Unless sink parameters are zeroed permanently, the
  weights still contain the source's knowledge; anyone with weight access can
  re-enable the mask (mask is deterministic from the source id). Claims should
  be read as *deployment-time* unlearning; the permanent-zeroing variant is
  mentioned but not evaluated for side effects.
- **Scale and breadth:** 1B models only; two source definitions; Wikipedia +
  C4/HP only. Whether the sink dynamic survives 8B+/longer training or
  instruction tuning is open (the authors flag post-training as future work).
- **Truth-ratio ceiling:** mean Sink-On TR on unique facts is ~1.9 at the
  default config — the model only modestly knows the facts being unlearned;
  distinctions could compress at higher-knowledge regimes (though Fig. 8b's
  N_source=250, TR≈5.8 point partially addresses this).
- GPT-5-generated cloze evals inherit whatever biases the generator has;
  distractor plausibility is unaudited.

## 8. Relation to prior work

- **Memorization Sinks (Ghosal et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.09937):** the direct
  predecessor — same architecture idea, but sink activation was per-sequence
  with no provenance mapping. NULLs' contribution is keying masks to source
  identity, making knowledge addressable and removable.
- **MoE/modular isolation (Gururangan et al. 2021 DEMix; Shi et al. 2025
  FlexOlmo):** disjoint experts per domain — clean removal, but parameters scale
  linearly in sources and no joint learning across them. NULLs shares the sink
  pool combinatorially.
- **Gradient routing (Cloud et al. 2024) / knowledge localization for
  capability removal (Shilov et al. 2025):** mask training gradients to route
  sources to parameter subsets; same coarse-granularity limits.
- **Post-hoc unlearning (NPO, Zhang et al. 2024; WHP, Eldan & Russinovich
  2023):** the baseline paradigm NULLs argues against, citing fragility results
  (Patil et al. 2023; Fan et al. 2025; quantization recovery, Zhang et al.
  2025).
- **HALO (this repo):** audits deletion in *external memory* (search-time
  filtering over a retrieval index). NULLs is the in-weights analog: the sink
  pool is a parametric memory keyed by source, and sink deactivation is the
  analog of index-side filtering. See §12.

## 9. Reproducibility

- Code: https://github.com/AR-FORUM/NULLS (litgpt-based `MemSinks/` package;
  `LLaMAMLPSeqTD` MLP class; training YAMLs; wiki-facts eval with cloze probes
  and TR; eval modes `all` / `dropout` / `activate_seq` with `exclude_seq_ids`).
- Checkpoints (HF, released 2026-08-24, litgpt `lit_model.pth` format, SmolLM2
  tokenizer):
  - `gauravrghosal/NULLS-Wikipedia-Full` — 12.1 GB; n_embd 960, 32 layers,
    MLP 8192, p_gen 0.061 (≈500 backbone), p_mem 0.013, `use_seqtd: true`.
  - `gauravrghosal/NULLS-HarryPotter` — 5.8 GB; MLP 2500, p_gen 0.2, p_mem 0.1.
- Eval data (cloze probes) and the article-title → source-id seeding convention
  live in the code repo; the exact Wikipedia dump snapshot is not stated on the
  HF cards. The bundled `config.json` is a standard LlamaForCausalLM config
  (intermediate 2560) that does NOT match the SeqTD checkpoint — loading via
  vanilla `transformers` will not reproduce the model; litgpt + their
  `SeqTDModel.py` is required.

## 10. Verdict

**Promising with caveats.** The mechanism is elegant, the retraining comparison
is the right gold standard and the adversarial results are strong, but
everything rests on single runs at 1B scale with likelihood-based metrics, and
the deployment story (routing, training-time source definitions, weight access)
carries untested assumptions.

## 11. Open questions

- How many retrained-from-scratch reference models back Fig. 4, and does the
  match hold across articles of varying frequency/popularity?
- Can the router be adversarially steered to activate a "deleted" sink?
- Does sink localization survive post-training (SFT/RLHF) or continued
  pretraining?
- What happens to unlearning quality for facts duplicated across few (2–5)
  sources — the gray zone between "unique" and "shared"?
- Permanent zeroing: collateral effects on overlapping masks (each sink neuron
  is shared by ~overlap-ratio fraction of sources)?

## 12. Relevance to HALO

NULLs is a natural **third deletion paradigm** for the cross-model suite:
Co-LMLM deletes by search-time filtering of an external index; SmolLM2/standard
LM are closed-book controls where deletion is undefined; NULLs is closed-book
with a *meaningful in-weights deletion operator*. The HALO state machine maps
almost one-to-one:

| HALO state | Co-LMLM meaning | NULLs analog |
|---|---|---|
| FULL | index intact, retrieval on | ground-truth article sink active (Sink-On) |
| DEL-ON | matching entries hidden, retrieval on | target sink excluded, routing live → next-closest sink (Sink-Off) |
| DEL-OFF | retrieval disabled | all sinks off, backbone only (their `dropout` mode) |

HALO metrics transfer directly: post-deletion survival ↔ Sink-Off recall of
target facts; unaided answerability ↔ backbone-only recall; collateral damage ↔
shared/inferred-fact degradation; deletion-radius sweeps ↔ deactivating the k
nearest source sinks by source-embedding distance (their routing embeddings give
the metric for free). This would let us report *the same audit table* for
memory-side and parameter-side deletion — a comparison neither paper makes.

Integration facts (for when we decide to add it):

- The Wikipedia model uses the SmolLM2-360M tokenizer and SmolLM2 backbone
  geometry — consistent with the suite's existing SmolLM2-360M and
  CoLMLM-Standard-LM-Baseline-360M-FW entries (but it is ~1B due to the 8192
  MLP, and trained on Wikipedia only, not FineWeb — not parameter- or
  data-matched to them).
- A `src/models/nulls_wiki/` backend implementing `AuditBackend.generate` would
  map DatabaseState → sink mode and emit a synthetic "retrieval trace" naming
  the activated sink(s); litgpt + their `SeqTDModel.py` needed as a vendored
  dependency (same pattern as the Co-LMLM adapter).
- **Main open blocker:** fact → source-id mapping. Sinks are keyed by Wikipedia
  article title. For T-REx facts the subject title usually equals the source
  article title, but the training dump snapshot is unknown, so title mismatches
  and redirects need auditing before cohort construction. Cohort rule would
  become "Sink-On answers correctly" instead of "retrieval mentions the answer".
- HALO's generation-based correctness (whole-phrase containment) is stricter
  than the paper's Truth Ratio; a 1B Wikipedia-only model may have low FULL
  accuracy under generation — worth a quick pilot (~100 prompts) before
  committing to a full run.
- No analog exists for HALO's adversarial-writes phase (no write path); the
  paraphrase/adversarial-prompt angle from the Cornell feedback maps instead to
  their GCG extraction setting.
- The fact-frequency/memorization confound raised in the July feedback maps
  cleanly onto their shared vs article-specific fact split — NULLs gives an
  architecture where that split is *causally manipulable* (activate k sinks),
  not just observable.
